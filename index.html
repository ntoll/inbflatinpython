What is this?

      

        It's a web-based art installation: a musical score, written in Python, that performs itself in the browser.
      

      
Why in B♭?

      

        In 1964 the American composer Terry Riley
        (non-SSL homepage) wrote "In C": fifty-three short musical phrases
        on a single page, to be played by any number of
        musicians, each repeating each phrase as many times as they please before moving on to the next. This isn't so
        much a composition as a set of instructions for wonderful community music-making. It was also the birth of
        musical minimalism.
      

      

        Inspired by "In C", in 2009 Darren Solomon
        invited musicians to video
        themselves playing in the key of B♭ and upload the results,
        so that any of the videos, started at any time and in
        any combination, would sound good together. The
        result, In Bb
        2.0, is a quietly wonderful corner of the
        pre-surveillance capitalist web, and its
        FAQ
        warmly invites remixing - a contemporary form of community music-making.
      

      

        This is such a remix with a focus on playful creative coding
        as a means of musical expression and experimentation. It's
        open source, and the code is the score, so you can modify it, and share your changes with others;
        yet another form of community music-making (through community coding).
      

      
How?

      

        Your Python code, in effect the musical score, makes
        you the conductor. Press Play and the performers enter
        the virtual stage, until the piece plays itself out; press
        Stop to return to the code. Randomness is a fun aspect of this,
        and defining the seed makes chance repeatable: the same
        seed is the same sequence of randomness every time. It guarantees that
        the same score will always produce the same performance so you can
        share your own remix with others.
      

      

        One performer speaks rather than plays:
        Information, a poem written and read by
        Daniel Donahoo. If the poem emerges, its
        words rise across the stage as it speaks.
      

      

        Under the bonnet there is no JavaScript written for
        this page at all: MicroPython runs your score in the
        browser via
        Browser Friendly
        Python, which also builds the stage, seats the
        performers, and listens for each one to finish. The
        editor is 
        CodeMirror. View source; it is all there.
      

      

        You have a lot of creative freedom:
        read the docs,
        see what you can do, and make something wonderful to
        share with others.
